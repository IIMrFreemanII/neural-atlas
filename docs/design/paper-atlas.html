Neural Atlas · UI proposal, round 5

Paper Atlas

An alternative look for Neural Atlas. Lessons sit on drafting paper like plates in a textbook, the visualization fills the window, and the narration lives on a card you can put away. Most frames are live mockups you can click, drag and turn; nothing in the app has changed yet.

HomeThe map is the front page

  • Built from the lesson page's parts: the map is the stage and the title sits on the same paper card.
  • Progress shows once, on the card. The map keeps only its key; the paper grid fades out behind it.
  • The top bar now has quick jump (⌘K) and a settings button next to the Paper / Night switch.
  • New: the map is a sheet lying on the desk with a soft shadow. It tilts a few degrees toward the pointer and the lessons float just above it; a dot lifts when you point at it.

First visitNothing done yet

  • With no progress the card says where to begin, how long the whole atlas takes and how lessons work.
  • The neuron is ringed as the start; every other dot is open. The primary button reads Start with the neuron.

LessonsChapters with figure plates

  • Each lesson row gets a small plate of its scene and its one-line summary.
  • Plates are today's link-preview images inverted as a stand-in; new ones come with the renderer work (a follow-up in the build plan).
  • New: pointing at a row lifts its plate off the page and tilts it toward the pointer (shown on Loss landscape until you move over the frame).

Lesson page · clickableTry it: this one works

  • Drag the sliders and the neuron recomputes. Click Back, Next or any segment of the step ruler; ← and → step too.
  • Hover a colored word or a key entry to highlight it in the figure. Open the deep dive; fold the card with its arrow.
  • Steps move with the motion rules below: values tween over 600 ms, text fades in, the card slides away.

Term highlightPoint at a word, see it in the figure

  • Hovering a colored term (here "weight") tints it in the text, bolds its key entry and fades everything else in the figure.
  • It works the other way too: hovering a key entry lights up the matching words in the text.

Deep diveMath and code get room

  • Opening the deep dive lays a wide sheet over the stage with math and code side by side, at reading size.
  • The figure stays visible behind it, dimmed; closing the sheet or pressing Escape brings it back.

ControlsEvery control kind, one family

  • Buttons, toggles, choices, text and sliders share one height, border and ink. The main action is solid and shows its Space shortcut.
  • Up to four choices show as a segmented row; more become a dropdown. Groups in a bar are split by a thin rule.
  • The last row shows two real bars: linear regression step 5 and tokenization.

Busy lessonSeven controls, four readouts

  • Backpropagation, last step. When the controls do not fit one row, the bar splits: actions and toggles on top, sliders below.
  • The figure shrinks to keep clear of the taller bar; four readouts still fit beside a four-line key.

QuestionQuestions live in the same card

  • The current question's diamond is ringed on the ruler. Choices are full-width rows.
  • After a right answer the row turns green with its explanation; the figure shows the learner's check with the slider.

Wrong answerA miss explains itself

  • The chosen row turns pink with the reason it is wrong. The right answer stays hidden so the learner can try again.
  • The sliders stay live, so the learner can test the idea before picking again.

TaskGoal, hint, solved

  • A task names its goal and shows where the figure is right now, so the learner sees how far off they are.
  • The hint opens in place. When the goal holds, the box turns green and the explanation appears.

Lesson completeA clear end and a next step

  • After the last page the card sums up the steps covered and questions solved, then offers the next lesson with its plate.

Chart lessonA figure with its own axes

  • Linear regression, step 4. The scene draws its own axes and grid; the paper grid fades out behind them so there is only one.
  • Squares sit on each miss in the gradient ink. The error bar shows the best possible error as a green tick.

3D scene · drag itA surface you can turn

  • Loss landscape, steps 4 to 7. Drag the figure to turn it; Back, Next or the step ruler glide the camera to each step's own angle over 800 ms.
  • The surface is line art: mesh lines, with hatching that gets denser where the surface faces away from the light. The ball is a matte object with a soft contact shadow.
  • New lines draw in like a pen stroke: the contours and the downhill arrow in step 5, the trail in steps 6 and 7. Run, Step and the learning rate work.

Points in space · drag itWords you can walk around

  • Embeddings as matte points standing on thin stems, each casting a soft shadow on a ruled floor, so height reads from any angle.
  • Step 2 rings each group on the floor; step 3 draws the man → woman and king → queen arrows, which stay parallel however you turn it.
  • Hover a word to see its three numbers. Show fades every group but one.

Layer stacks · drag themEvery layer is a sheet of paper

  • Networks with layers are drawn as paper sheets standing in space, each with a cast shadow on the desk. Sheets use an even projection (no vanishing point), like a technical drawing.
  • CNN feature maps walks through four steps: the picture, a filter sliding across it (press Scan), three feature maps, then pooling and scores (press Classify). Changing the digit recomputes every sheet.
  • The transformer block stacks its layers as floors with one column per token; diffusion lines snapshots up from noise to picture.

Signal pulses · press TrainWatch the signal travel

  • When a value is computed, dots run along the connections: teal going forward, orange going back as blame. Each lap ends with one weight update and the readouts change.
  • Pulses run only when something is computed (a step, a button, a slider let go), never as idle decoration. The clickable neuron lesson (D) sends a pulse on every change too.

PlaygroundFree play with a small network

  • Its own page with the same stage: the network on the left, its answer for every point of the plane on the right.
  • The card becomes the setup: data, network shape, activation, learning rate and the loss curve. Train, Step and Reset sit in the bar.

Put away and ContentsAll stage, or the whole list

  • The card folds into a tab that still shows the step; the figure re-centres in the full width.
  • Contents slides the lesson list over the stage, with the current track open.

SettingsMotion and theme in one menu

  • The gear next to the Paper / Night switch opens a small menu: follow the system theme, reduce motion, and the keyboard shortcuts.
  • Reduce motion keeps today's behavior: it follows the system until the learner picks a side, and the choice is saved in this browser.

Quick jump⌘K finds lessons, steps and terms

  • One search over lesson titles, step titles and glossary terms, grouped, with the match marked.
  • Arrow keys move, Enter opens, Escape closes. Opening a step jumps straight to it inside its lesson.

GlossaryA dictionary in the same ink

  • Search and a letter index on the left; entries read like a dictionary, with the term in its lesson color.
  • Each entry lists the lessons that use it. Following a term chip from a lesson lands on its entry, tinted (here "bias").

NightPaper, with the lights off

  • Exactly the Paper design; only the color tokens change. No glow or bloom in either theme.
  • Plates show the original dark previews, without the inversion used on paper.
  • 3D figures keep the same inks; shadows get deeper and the paper grain turns light, so depth still reads on the dark sheet.

MotionWhat moves, and for how long

  • Motion explains change: values glide so the eye can follow cause and effect. Nothing moves just to decorate.
  • The 600 ms ease-in-out tween is today's app behavior, kept as is. The clickable lesson above uses these rules.
